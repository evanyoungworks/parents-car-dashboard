How we figured this

Each year is 14,959 miles. That is 11,071 miles of ordinary driving, the recent U.S. average, plus four trips from Kansas City to Madison, Wisconsin and back. Each trip is 486 miles one way, so the four round trips are 3,888 highway miles.

$4.13 a gallon is the starting Kansas City price for regular. The slider above changes it. Ordinary miles use the combined miles per gallon. The Madison trips use the highway miles per gallon.

Charging at home is 15.7 cents per kilowatt-hour. Charging on the Madison trips is about 34 cents per kilowatt-hour in a Tesla, and 48 cents in a Kia electric SUV.

Insurance is $1,812 a year for every car. It is included in the 7-year total, but it does not change which car is cheaper.

There is no sales tax, no Missouri personal property tax, and no charger. The purchase is cash, with no loan.

Maintenance is an average for that kind of car, not a repair quote for this exact one. A gas SUV is about 11 cents a mile, a hybrid about 9 cents, and an electric car about 8 cents. On the Model Y, that average is probably high. Evan's own 2023 Model Y has only needed one set of tires.

Each year the car is worth a little less than the year before, starting from today's price. A gas SUV loses about 11% a year, a hybrid about 8%, most electric cars about 16%, a Model Y about 16%, and a Model X about 17%. After 7 years we count what it has lost, not the whole purchase price.

The 7-year total is the value it loses, plus 7 years of fuel, maintenance, and insurance. Dollars are rounded to the nearest dollar. If a listing did not state the gas mileage, the car's card says what we used.
